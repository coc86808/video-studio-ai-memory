What if you owned the Brooklyn Bridge and charged every person a toll?

That was the pitch. And people bought it.

Meet George C. Parker — the most brazen con artist in American history.

In the early 1900s, Parker would approach newly arrived immigrants near the Brooklyn Bridge. He posed as the official owner — a city developer with government ties.

He had forged deeds. Stamped legal documents. Official-looking certificates of ownership. And a smooth, confident voice that made you feel like YOU were the one getting a secret deal.

The price? Anywhere from fifty dollars to several thousand. A fortune for most immigrants.

And they paid. Dozens of them did.

The scam only unraveled when buyers actually showed up at the bridge with lumber and tools — ready to install their toll booths.

NYPD officers would arrive to find ordinary people, hammer in hand, trying to barricade one of the world's most famous bridges.

But Parker didn't stop at the Brooklyn Bridge. He also sold Madison Square Garden. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. And Grant's Tomb.

He pulled this off for DECADES before finally getting caught and sentenced to Sing Sing prison for life.

His legacy? The classic American expression — "If you believe that, I have a bridge to sell you."

Greed makes even the smartest people miss the most obvious lies.
