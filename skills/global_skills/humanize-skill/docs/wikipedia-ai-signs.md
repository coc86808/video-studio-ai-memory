# Field Guide: Wikipedia Signs of AI Writing (WP:AISIGNS)

Based on Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).

## 1. Content Tells
- **Undue emphasis on significance, legacy, and broader trends**: Claiming everything 'stands as a testament', 'marks a turning point', or 'shapes the modern landscape'.
- **Superficial analyses & empty generalizations**: Writing generic summaries that sound profound but contain zero specific technical or physical facts.
- **Promotional / puffery language**: Using inflated descriptors ('renowned', 'groundbreaking', 'prestigious', 'vibrant').
- **Vague attributions**: Phrases like 'many experts agree', 'widely regarded as', 'critics have pointed out'.
- **Outline-like conclusions**: Formulaic wrap-ups discussing 'challenges and future prospects' or 'looking ahead'.

## 2. Language & Grammar Tells
- **High density of AI vocabulary words**:
  - *Verbs*: delve, foster, bolster, harness, navigate, showcase, underscore, encapsulate, intertwine, resonate, illuminate.
  - *Nouns*: tapestry, beacon, testament, landscape, realm, interplay, nexus, cornerstone, symphony.
  - *Adjectives*: multifaceted, crucial, pivotal, holistic, vital, vibrant, profound, paramount, enduring.
- **Avoidance of basic copulatives**: Substituting 'is', 'are', 'was' with 'serves as', 'stands as', 'acts as', 'functions as', 'boasts', 'features'.
- **Negative parallelisms**:
  - 'Not just X, but also Y'
  - 'Not X, but Y'
  - 'Y rather than X'
- **Rule of Three**: Compulsively listing items in groups of three ('safety, efficiency, and reliability').

## 3. Style Tells
- **Overuse of em-dashes (—)**: Inserting side comments or participial clauses with em-dashes where human writers use commas, periods, parentheses, or separate sentences.
- **Uniform sentence length & rhythm**: Writing entire paragraphs where every sentence has 18–25 words with no short fragments or long conversational flows.
- **Formulaic transitional phrases**: 'Beyond X...', 'When it comes to...', 'In addition to...', 'Above all...'.

## 4. Operational Invariants for Human Writing
1. Never use formulaic transitions or AI vocabulary.
2. Use plain, direct verbs ('is', 'has', 'does', 'cuts') instead of copulative substitutes.
3. Vary sentence length radically (mix 3-to-6 word sentences with longer explanations).
4. State direct observations without inflating them into societal or philosophical milestones.
